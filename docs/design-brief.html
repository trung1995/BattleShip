Battleship redesign brief

A single-player Battleship game against the computer. All the game logic works and is tested. The visuals are developer placeholders: flat shapes in code, one font and no artwork. We want a complete visual identity and an asset set we can drop into the engine.

The game

What the player does

The player and the computer each hide a fleet of 10 ships on a 10 × 10 grid: one 4-cell ship, two 3-cell, three 2-cell and four 1-cell ships. Ships run horizontally or vertically and may not touch, not even at the corners.

They take turns firing at cells on each other's grid. A hit earns another shot and a miss passes the turn. When every cell of a ship has been hit the ship is sunk, and the cells around it are marked automatically as "no ship here". The first side to sink the whole enemy fleet wins.

Each side also has one Help per game: a cross-shaped strike that fires at a centre cell and the cells directly above, below, left and right of it (up to 5 cells), then ends the turn.

A session is short (5–15 minutes), and the computer has three difficulty levels: Easy, Medium and Hard.

Scope

What is fixed and what is yours

Fixed
  • 10 × 10 grid, with both axes labelled with the numerals 1–10 (no letters, so they read the same in every language).
  • The fleet composition and the game rules.
  • Design canvas of 1280 × 720. Other window shapes are letterboxed, so keep everything important inside this frame.
  • Mouse-driven: drag and drop to place ships, right-click to rotate, left-click to fire.
  • No text inside images. All text comes from translation files and is drawn live by the engine, so a language switch updates it instantly.
  • Every element listed in the screen section below must stay on its screen.
Open to you
  • The whole art direction: mood, era, palette, materials (modern naval UI, vintage paper chart, toy-like, and so on).
  • Layout and positions on every screen, and the button order.
  • Board and cell size. Today a cell is 40 px; anything from about 34 to 48 px fits two boards side by side.
  • Ship artwork, the hit, miss and sunk effects, and the background art.
  • Fonts, as long as they cover Latin, Cyrillic and Vietnamese (see Typography).
  • Optional short animations (see Motion).

Screens

Screen by screen

Each map shows the current placeholder layout at true 1280 × 720 proportions. It is an inventory of what must be on the screen, not a layout to keep. Dashed yellow outlines mark areas where text appears and changes length.

1 · Main menu

  • The game title, New Game, Settings and Quit.
  • This is the first impression and the best place for key art (a fleet, the sea, a chart table).

2 · Difficulty overlay (on top of the menu)

  • Heading "Choose difficulty", buttons Easy, Medium, Hard and Back.
  • Optional: one line under each level describing the opponent (we can add the text).

3 · Settings

  • Sound effects on/off toggle and Music on/off toggle.
  • Language selector with three options. Their names always appear in their own language: English, Русский, Tiếng Việt.
  • Player name text field: up to 20 characters, any script, with a placeholder text and a focused state with a caret.
  • Back button.

4 · Fleet placement

The green 2-cell ship shows a legal drop preview. Start battle stays disabled until all 10 ships are placed.

  • The player's board, a dock of unplaced ships grouped by length with a remaining count (×4, ×3…), and a hint line.
  • Buttons: Rotate, Auto-place remaining, Auto-place all, Start battle (disabled and enabled states), Back.
  • While dragging, a ghost ship follows the cursor and the board shows a preview: legal (green today) or illegal (red today) with a written reason.
  • A placed ship can be selected (to rotate it) and dragged again. Dropping it on the dock removes it.

5 · Battle

Left: the player's own fleet. Right: the enemy board with the yellow Help cross preview following the cursor.

  • Own board (left): the player's ships with the computer's shots on them. Target board (right): only what has been revealed; hidden enemy ships are never drawn.
  • Name and caption above each board, and a turn banner: "Your turn" or "Computer is firing…". The computer fires every 0.8 s, so the player should always see whose turn it is at a glance.
  • Help button with three states: available, armed (targeting) and used. While armed, a cross preview follows the cursor over the target board, cut off at the board edges, with already-fired cells shown as skipped.
  • Menu button, which opens the leave dialog.
  • Hover state for target cells and a "not allowed" cue for cells already fired at.

Overlays

  • Leave battle? A modal panel with title, one line of text, and Stay / Leave buttons, over a dimmed battle. Leave is the destructive choice.
  • Game over: "{name} wins!" with New game and Main menu buttons, over the final board state, which stays visible. It needs a victory variant and a defeat variant (the music and sounds already differ).
  • Toast: a short message near the bottom, such as "Ships cannot overlap" or "You have already fired at this cell". It fades after a moment.

Cell states

These are the core of the game's readability. Every state must be distinguishable at a glance, including by colour-blind players, so use shape as well as colour.

Text

Typography and text length

The font must cover Latin, Cyrillic and Vietnamese, including Vietnamese stacked diacritics such as ế, ữ, ặ. Those need extra line height so they don't clip. The font licence must allow embedding in a distributed game (OFL is ideal). We currently use Noto Sans Regular and Bold at three sizes: 44, 24 and 18 px.

Russian and Vietnamese run 30–60% longer than English. Size buttons and banners for the longest version below. Where text would wrap, design for two lines.

Motion

Animation (optional, nice to have)

  • Shot impact: a splash for a miss and an explosion for a hit, each under 0.6 s, because the computer fires every 0.8 s.
  • Sinking: a slightly bigger moment, about 1 s at most.
  • Help strike: the cross lighting up across the board.
  • Button hover and press, a ship lifting while dragged, and a victory or defeat flourish.
  • Deliver as PNG sprite sheets or frame sequences with timing notes, or describe them (scale, fade, shake) and we build them in code.
